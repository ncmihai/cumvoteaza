### Q21 — Sorting the members list by activity or absences
- The members list can be sorted by "most documented votes" and "most documented absences". D-029 says no rankings, leaderboards or "best / worst" lists of individuals; sorting everyone by absences is exactly such a ranking, and a count of absences also mixes real absences with gaps in a source.

- 2026-10-06: at the owner's request the absence sort now counts real absences (votes with a name list held in the member's chamber and in joint sittings during the mandate, minus the votes where the member has a recorded vote), only for the current legislature, with the meaning written above the list and a marker on members who held a government post (they top the list: Buzoianu 974 of 1,317, Țoiu 941, Miruță 934). It is still a ranking of individuals, so the question stays open: keep, or remove as recommended.

### D-042 — The Presidents' pages and the appointments by office (2026-10-10, Sprint 18, first part)
- **A President is a signer.** Each page runs from the first to the last decree we hold for that signer, so the dates are those of the decrees and not of the term (the page says so); an acting President (a signature "interimar", Bolojan in 2025) has a page of his own. Nothing about a term is typed in by hand: no date or name on these pages comes from memory.
- **"Elected in" is derived, not written:** the second round in our presidential results whose winner has the signer's name (2009, 2014, 2025). Iohannis's second term (2019) has no election in our data yet and says so.
- **The route of each office is written from the law's text, read on the legislative portal, and only then.** The Constitution (republished 2003): art. 142 (Constitutional Court: nine judges, three each by the Chamber, the Senate and the President), art. 85 and 103 (the Government, the prime-minister candidate), art. 91(2) (ambassadors, on the Government's proposal), art. 94 (appointments to public office as the law provides), art. 65(2)(h) (the intelligence services' directors, appointed by the chambers in joint sitting on the President's proposal); Law 303/2004 art. 53 and 54 (the High Court's leaders on the Council's proposal; the Prosecutor General, DNA and DIICOT chiefs and their deputies on the Minister of Justice's proposal with the Council's opinion), as consolidated on 3 September 2022; Law 14/1992 art. 23 (the SRI director); Law 1/1998 (the SIE director: first by the Supreme Council of National Defence, amended in 2017). An office whose route we did not read is not listed. The summaries are paraphrases; the links go to the texts.
- **"Appointed by the President" is never written for a nomination.** The SRI and SIE directors are appointed by Parliament in joint sitting on the President's proposal: they have an office page with the route and no names (Parliament's decisions are not read yet). What the President does decide alone, or on someone's proposal, is marked by the route label of each office.
- **What is read from a decree stays D-037's:** names only from the six kinds that concern a public office; never from decorations, pardons, ordinary judges and prosecutors, or military ranks. For the ranks the page gives only the **number of decrees per institution** (SRI, SIE, SPP, STS, the defence and interior ministries), read from the title, never a name.
- **Each person a decree names has an action, an office and a title** (migration 0059), read from the sentence by plain phrases with 27 tested cases; a sentence no phrase knows is "other". The decree's whole sentence is read, not the 300 characters we keep: an ambassador's sentence names every country before the verb.
- **Parliament's decisions are read from the portal's public search by the words of their titles** (issuer Parliament, the Chamber or the Senate; type "hotărâre") and each page is saved byte for byte; the person is read from the operative sentence, as the decision prints the name (often the surname first, sometimes with a title: "Domnul senator X", "Domnul X, profesor universitar, ... se numește"). A decision whose page has no text on the portal (one of 1995) is reported, never guessed. They fill `parliament_appointments` and the office pages of the SRI and SIE directors and of the Constitutional Court (the nine seats: three by the President's decrees, the others by the chambers' decisions).
- **Photographs come from Wikimedia Commons only, with the author and the licence on the page** (public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA; any other tag, such as a generic "Attribution" or a legal notice, is refused until its terms are read). A portrait is chosen by hand from the person's Commons category when Wikidata's own image is not under such a licence, cropped to a head and shoulders if the picture needs it, and shrunk to 360 px (`tools/photos/build-figures.py`, `figures.config.json`, `lib/figures.json`). A person without a photograph shows initials; no picture is attached to a name by resemblance. The same rule for the other people: only an exact name plus birth date, or a unique name with a matching occupation, once built.
- **Decrees before 2014:** the portal holds 19,558 for 1990 to 2013 (a probe of 24 requests); the lists for 2004 to 2013 are being saved, and the texts (for the signers and the names) follow. The Băsescu page therefore covers only 2014 until the owner imports them.

### D-041 — The election map and the presidents' pages: the choices (2026-10-10, Sprint 17 built, Sprint 18 planned)
- **Our own shapes, no map service.** SVG drawn in the browser from two small static files; no tiles, no third-party request, no map library on the page (d3-geo and topojson are dev dependencies of the build script only). **The boundaries are not Eurostat's:** GISCO's commune boundaries (LAU 2024, whose code is the SIRUTA code) are free for non-commercial use only and want the credit "© EuroGeographics" on every output; commercial use is licensed by EuroGeographics, and open downloads later (D-026) would redistribute a derivative. The commune borders are **ANCPI's public data, processed by geo-spatial.org** ("UAT, România (poligon, geometrie simplificată)", version 26 March 2025, CC BY 4.0 in the catalogue record, the SIRUTA code in `natCode`), credited on the map page and the methodology page. Matches: all 3,186 shapes against the 3,186 communes of 2016 and 2024; 2020 needed two codes brought to the current one (Bucharest's sectors, Băneasa in Constanța). The county layer is the communes merged; counties of the HDX/OCHA set (CC BY-IGO) were the alternative.
- **Votes per commune are stored as two integer arrays per election, chamber and commune** (list codes and votes; about 20,000 rows, 7 MB) with a small `election_lists` table for the codes, not one row per list (about 570,000), because the database is close to its size limit (feedback of 6 Oct). The votes by mail belong to no place and are not in these rows.
- **Turnout is column b over column a1 (voters who came over the permanent lists), the same in every election.** Over all lists (a) it is lower, and which one the press uses differs by year; the page states the definition and one example.
- **Unknown stays unknown:** 2024 has no mandates until an official distribution is found; every map metric that needs data we do not hold is absent, not zero, and a commune whose code finds no shape is listed with its votes, not dropped.
- **An appointment is shown with its route.** The President names some office-holders by decree, proposes others to Parliament, signs decrees for people proposed by others, and has no part in the rest. The page for each office says which of these it is, with the article of the law, and never writes "appointed by the President" for a nomination. A route is written only after the law's text is read.
- **A decree belongs to the president in office on its date** (the term table), and the signature is the check; the interim presidents have their own pages. Decorations, individual pardons and the names of ordinary judges and prosecutors stay out (D-021, D-029).
- **Presidential rounds are elections of their own** (`pres-2025-r2`, `pres-2025-r1`, `pres-2024-r1`) in the same three tables, with the ballot "president" (a `ballot` enum, because adding a value to the chamber enum breaks eleven typed places) and the candidates as the lists; `elections.kind` keeps them off the parliamentary tables and the party pages. The annulled first round of 24 November 2024 is shown, with the Constitutional Court's Decision 32/2024 as its note, not hidden. Their files come from the portal by hand, as in D-040. The older ones (2009 and 2014, the AEP's spreadsheets on data.gov.ro, one row per polling station with the SIRUTA code and the candidates' votes) are old `.xls` files: `tools/xlsx/legacy-presidential.py` (needs `xlrd`, installed in a throwaway virtual environment, not in the project) turns them into the portal's layout, mapping the county numbers (checked against the 2025 names; sectors 42 to 47 become 44 to 49, abroad 48 becomes 43) and leaving out the 2014 file's "Total" row; the importer then reads them like the others. All 3,186 communes of 2014 and 3,185 of 2009 match a shape. Their totals are the official ones (2009: Băsescu 3,153,640 in round 1 and 5,275,808 in round 2; 2014: Ponta 3,836,093 and Iohannis 6,288,769). There were no votes by mail before 2019, so these have no mail file. The candidates' names are the files' own (the 2009 file has no diacritics); the map of change for a second round compares with the first round of the same election, for a first round with the first round before it. 2019: no dataset found on data.gov.ro.
- **Gaps stay gaps:** presidency.ro (browser check) is not read; what only it holds (agenda, statements, Supreme Defence Council decisions) is labelled as missing.

### D-040 — The 2024 elections: the AEP's own files, put in by hand (2026-10-10, Sprint 15 follow-up)
- **Owner's call:** Code for Romania's "Rezultate Vot" (an NGO project, not the government; its open API at `api.rezultatevot.ro/api` covers 1996 to the June 2024 locals and European elections and is not used) might carry licence trouble, so the official source is preferred.
- **The official source answers programs with a browser check** (`prezenta.roaep.ro` gave HTTP 418 again on 10 Oct), which we do not get past. The way in is a person: **the maintainer downloads the files in a browser** from the portal's pages ("Rezultate finale", the minutes of the polling stations, national level) and drops them in `data/manual/elections/parl-2024/` as `deputies.csv` and `senate.csv` (and `deputies-mandates.csv`, `senate-mandates.csv` if the portal offers the distribution of mandates). Press and NGO accreditation to the portal's file feed (`/presa/pv`) is the other route and is the AEP's to grant.
- **The format is known** from Code for Romania's backup repository `code4romania/romanian-elections-data` (MPL 2.0 for the repository; it holds only the June 2024 European and the 2024 and 2025 local elections, so it does not fill the gap): one row per polling station, `precinct_county_nce`, `precinct_county_name`, the report columns, the statistics a to f, then one column per list "<LIST>-voturi". County codes 1 to 41 and 43 are the circumscription numbers of 2016 and 2020; Bucharest is sectors 44 to 49 and becomes 42. The reader (`sumPvListVotes`) was checked on the European 2024 national file: 8,942,629 list votes, PSD-PNL alliance 48.55%, AUR 14.93%, USR-PMP-FD 8.71%, UDMR 6.48%, equal to the official result.
- **Mandates are optional:** without a mandates file the election is stored with `mandates_known = false` and the pages show votes only, never a zero. The importer skips the election until its files are there (`elections:import` says which one is waiting).
- **Done 10 Oct:** the owner downloaded the Chamber and Senate minutes files and the two mail files in a browser; imported on dev and production (6,165 rows for the three elections); Chamber PSD 21.96%, AUR 18.01%, PNL 13.20%, USR 12.40%; Senate PSD 22.30%, AUR 18.30%. No mandates file, so `mandates_known = false` for 2024.
- **2012 (added 10 Oct):** the AEP's 2012 files from data.gov.ro, saved by the owner in his browser (the certificate there had expired): the circumscription spreadsheet (votes of each competitor by chamber) and the candidates' spreadsheet ("Mandat Atribuit"), converted to CSV by `tools/xlsx/xls-to-csv.py` (xlrd, a throwaway virtual environment) and read by `readLegacy2012`. Checks: 412 deputies and 176 senators; Uniunea Social Liberală 4,344,288 votes and 273 seats in the Chamber, 4,457,526 and 122 in the Senate. The 2012 polling-station file names the commune but has no SIRUTA code, so 2012 has no map by commune (a match by name would be a guess); it is on the elections page and the party pages, with a note.
- **Licence:** official election results are public records; the 2024 files carry no licence text we can see (data.gov.ro files for 2016 and 2020 do: CC BY 4.0 and OGL-ROU-1.0), so the page names the AEP as the source and the date the file was downloaded, and the AEP is asked in writing if there is doubt.

### D-037 — The President's decrees (2026-10-09, Sprint 14)
- **The portal's web service loses decrees; its public search does not.** A first catalog through the SOAP service (title word "decret", ten acts a page, newest first) held about 60% of what exists: the sort key is the date, so decrees of one day change places between requests, and a search by number answers a different subset for each shape of query (the year filter is ignored with a title filter). The portal's **public search page** (`/Public/RezultateCautare?tipdoc=3&sectiuneact=49&semnatinceputtext=...`; 3 is DECRET, 49 the President of Romania) takes its criteria in the address, needs no session, counts the documents (**16,886 signed since 1 January 2014**) and lists them 50 to a page. The catalog is read **month by month** and each month is checked against the portal's own count; a month that does not add up is read again with smaller pages. The SOAP pages already saved add the text and the signature where they have it.
- **The text of a decree is never stored** (it names decorated private persons, D-029 Q19) and only the title, number, date, Official Gazette and portal page go to `presidential_decrees`. A page of the portal is saved byte for byte for the decrees that need their text: the six office-holding kinds, and the first and last decree of each month.
- **Type from the title, by plain rules** (promulgation, designation of the prime-minister candidate, Government and ministers, judges and prosecutors, judiciary leadership, Constitutional Court, ambassadors and diplomacy, armed forces, presidential advisers, decorations, pardons, emergency and troops, Parliament and referendum, corrections of decrees, other). Returns of a law for re-examination and referrals to the Constitutional Court are **not decrees** (they are letters to Parliament) and are not in this catalog; they belong to the bill dossiers.
- **Who signed** is read from the signature at the end of the decree ("PREȘEDINTELE ROMÂNIEI - interimar - ILIE-GAVRIL BOLOJAN"); a decree the Prime Minister countersigns closes with "În temeiul art. 100 alin. (2)... contrasemnăm". A decree whose text we do not hold gets its signer from the decrees signed just before and after it when both have the same signer in the same capacity, marked as inferred on the page; across a change of President it gets none.
- **Appointments register (closes Q7, as built):** the people a decree names are read only from six kinds that concern a public office (ministers, the prime-minister candidate, ambassadors, Constitutional Court judges, the heads of the judiciary, presidential advisers), each with the sentence of the decree that names them, linked to a member page when the name, written as a slug, is exactly one of our people. **Never read for names:** decorations, pardons, judges and prosecutors, military ranks. Names of ordinary judges and prosecutors, and of the decorated, stay out (D-021, D-029).
- **Not read:** presidency.ro (the agenda, statements, the Supreme Defence Council, advisers' page) answers HTTP 503 and a browser check to programs.

### D-036 — MP enrichment: what the official pages print (2026-10-09, Sprint 13 source check and 13a)
- **Date of birth is already in the pages we saved.** The header of every Chamber profile ("Cauta Mirela Elena Adomnicăi n. 15 aug. 1970") prints it, for deputies and for senators alike (cdep.ro keeps a profile for every senator too): 4,973 of the 5,286 saved profile pages, 3,081 of 3,352 people, **459 of the 464 sitting members** (the other five print none: two headers carry no date, two profiles were never saved). The plan's CV crawl is therefore not needed for this; the 0 of 3,366 in the plan was only that nobody had read the header. Format: day, abbreviated month (ian., feb., ..., noi., dec.; "mai" in full), year.
- **The Senate's own card is not used.** It keeps the date and the place of birth inside a commented-out HTML block that its page does not show (`<!--Data naşterii: ... Locul naşterii: ...-->`); the Senate chose not to display them, so we do not read what it hid. The senators' dates come from the Chamber's profile pages, which print them openly.
- **Disagreements are listed, never resolved (owner's rule).** A person whose profiles print different dates gets no date. Five people do (Constantin Gheorghe, Vasile Matei, Dumitru Badea, Nicolae Enescu, Ioan Munteanu), and in each the dates are 6 to 42 years apart, so these are most probably two different people the identity step merged under one name. They stay listed in the import report; splitting them is an identity repair of its own, not done here.
- **Stored in `person_biographies`** (person, date, the profile page it comes from, read date; migration 0048), shown on the member page as "Data nașterii" with a "sursă" link. Marital status and children are never stored (D-021).
- **Official counts for deputies** come from the "Activitatea parlamentara în cifre" block of the 2024 profile (332 deputies): speeches (with the number of sittings), political declarations, legislative initiatives (with how many became law), questions and interpellations (the Chamber publishes them as one number), motions. A line the page does not print is left out, never written as zero. They go to `member_official_activity` as published, beside our own counts, with the date the page was read.
- **CVs.** The CV page of a deputy is `structura.mp?...&pag=0`; 260 of the 336 deputies of 2024 link one (and 20 of the 137 senators on cdep.ro; the Senate's own biography sits behind a form post and is not read yet). The page is the Europass layout or a free text, with "Data naşterii", professional, political and parliamentary career.
- **Questions and interpellations (deputies).** `interpelari.lista?tip=A&dat=<year>` lists the year's questions (2026 so far: 2,888) and `tip=B` its interpellations (908), each a link to `interpelari.detalii?idi=<n>`; the detail page names the deputy and party, the addressee (ministry and minister), the dates, the PDF of the text and, when there is one, the answer with its date, its number, who answered and its PDF. The list rows carry only the number, the date and the title, so the addressee needs the detail page (about 9,800 requests for the legislature).
- **Sanctions.** The profile pages carry no sanctions section (the only match of "sanc" is "sancţionarea violenţei domestice", a committee). Their source is still to be found.

### D-035 — Amendments in committee reports: what the sample of 25 found (2026-10-09, Sprint 12d, sample only)
- **Sample:** 25 committee-report PDFs of 2025-2026 (11 from the Chamber's committees on cdep.ro, 14 from the Senate's on senat.ro; 30 asked for, 5 plain-`http` cdep.ro addresses did not connect, the real run must use https), chosen at random among the reports that state amendments and some that do not; saved under `data/coverage/raw/committee-report`. Counts since January 2025: **714 distinct Chamber report PDFs and 1,069 Senate ones**; the Chamber's dossier rows state the number of amendments admitted or rejected for 191 of the 714 (27%), the Senate's for **none** of the 1,069, so for a Senate report the PDF is the only source.
- **Text extraction works for the Chamber's reports and is poor for the Senate's.** Chamber reports are born-digital: a clean text layer in 11 of 11 (0.0 to 0.8% garbled tokens). Senate reports are scans with an embedded OCR layer: 14 of 14 have text, but 9 of the 14 have more than 2% garbled tokens and several have broken diacritics and names ("Sdndor", "Crfstici", "imunită~i i validări"), so a person's name cannot be read from them with confidence. The long ones are not scanned throughout (a 65-page joint report had a clean amendment annex).
- **The amendment annex is not one format.** The Chamber's committees print "AMENDAMENTE ADMISE" or "RESPINSE" as an annex table, but each with its own headings ("Anexă Amendamente admise", "Amendament Admis"), its own columns (text in force, text of the bill, committee's text, reasoning) and its own way of naming the author ("Autori: ... deputat USR", "(Amendament propus de domnul deputat ... – deputat PSD)", "Amendament / Autor", "(autorul amendamentului)"); 6 of the 10 Chamber reports that state amendments had a table annex, the others describe them in the text. A generic per-amendment reading would guess; what can be read without guessing is: **that the report has an annex of admitted and/or rejected amendments, and on which page** (so the bill page can link the annex itself), and **the authors named in it** (a name with "deputat" or "senator" and a group, not tied to a particular amendment). The Senate's reports stay "not read" (their count is unknown from the dossier and their text is not reliable), with the share stated.
- **No OCR path is needed for the Chamber's reports.** The Mac Vision OCR in `tools/pdf-ocr` (slow, 120 s a file) would be the way to improve the Senate's, and only makes sense if reading their annexes is wanted; that is the Sprint 10 backlog item.

### D-034 — "Prioritate legislativă": what the Senate's bulletin prints (2026-10-09, Sprint 12c)
- **The plan said "the Government's priority list, Senate PDFs per session".** The source check found something more precise and less than that. The Senate's legislative department publishes a **Legislative Bulletin** for each ordinary session (listed at senat.ro under Resurse parlamentare, Procesul legislativ, Buletin legislativ; five PDFs of 150 to 420 pages from February-June 2024 to February-June 2026; the autumn 2026 session has none yet, it is issued after the session). Every bill registered at the Senate that session, and every bill it adopted, has an entry with its number ("L488/2024", from 2025 "L29/2025 / PLX71/2025 / E16/2025"), its labels ("procedură de urgenţă", "lege ordinară" or "lege organică") and, for some, **"prioritate legislativă"**. The bulletin does **not** say who asked for the label, and the Government's own priority lists are on sgg.gov.ro, a different source we have not read. So the site says exactly what the Senate prints: the label is printed by the Senate in its Legislative Bulletin, for the session X (page N), and the bulletin does not say who asked for it. It is never called "the Government's priority".
- **Read from the PDF text** (the same pypdf reader the bill-text importer uses; `tools/pdf-text/pages.py`): the labels of a bill are the words between its own number and the next bill's number (right after it in 2024, after the stage in 2025 and 2026). **Result:** 39 flags on 38 bills over the five sessions (8, 11, 2, 14 and 4), every one matched to a stored bill by its Senate number; 33 of the 38 are promulgated laws, the rest in progress. The counts agree with how often the label is printed in each PDF (each flagged bill is listed once among those registered and once among those adopted).
- **Stored:** `bill_priority_flags (bill_id, session, bulletin address and page, senate number, urgency, law kind)` (migration 0046), replaced per session on each import. The PDFs are saved byte for byte under `data/coverage/raw/senate-bulletin` (5 requests, 4 s apart). The bill page shows an amber "Prioritate legislativă" chip and the sentence above, linking to the bulletin PDF at that page (`#page=N`).
- **Commands:** `ingest:bills:priorities:fetch --live`, `ingest:bills:priorities:import --persist`. A new bulletin appears after each session; add its address to `SENATE_BULLETINS` (`packages/ingest/src/dossiers/priorities.ts`) and run both again.
- **Not done:** the Government's own lists (sgg.gov.ro), and the flag on the bills list.

### D-033 — The ordinance behind an approval bill (2026-10-09, Sprint 12b)
- **Which ordinance** is read from the bill's title, never guessed: the words "aprobarea Ordonanţei [de urgenţă a] Guvernului nr. N/YYYY" (467 of the 467 approval bills in the database match, 429 distinct ordinances; none approves two in one title). The title goes on to name what the ordinance amends, and those are not the one approved. The Chamber's titles spell "urgenţã" with a tilde-a; diacritics are folded away so both read the same.
- **Where it comes from:** the legislative portal's own web service (legislatie.just.ro, the one the gazette numbers already use). What is stored of an ordinance: its number, year and kind (OUG or OG), its date, title, issuer, the Official Gazette number and date it was published in, and the address of its text on the portal. The text itself is not copied: the card links to it.
- **Matching is strict.** The portal answers a number with every act of that number in any year and of any type, and its title filter and paging are erratic (the same page twice, an act found by one word of its title and not by another). So an answer counts only when the type (urgency or ordinary), the number and the **year of the act's own date** all agree; the lookup tries the number and type first, then up to six distinctive words of the ordinance's title, and saves the act's own record byte for byte (or a note that it was not found) so nothing is asked twice. A bill whose ordinance the portal does not return keeps the reference from the title and says so; no date is invented.
- **Tables** (migration 0045): `government_ordinances` (one row per ordinance) and `bill_ordinances` (the reference from each bill's title, with the ordinance when found). **Commands:** `ingest:bills:ordinances:fetch --live` (the polite crawl, one request every 2 s, resumable; owner or agent on the owner's machine) and `ingest:bills:ordinances:import --persist` (offline). The bill page shows an "Ordonanța aprobată prin acest proiect" card above the reports and opinions: kind, number, date, title, gazette issue, the link to the text and the copy the Government filed with Parliament when the dossier lists one.
- **Not done:** the content of the ordinance, whether Parliament approved it with changes (the bill's fate and the law number are on the bill page), and ordinances amended by other ordinances.

### D-032 — Every document of a dossier step, and the Reports and opinions panel (2026-10-09, Sprint 12a)
- **Source check first** (the 2,830 saved dossier pages, nothing fetched). The plan assumed the reports and opinions had no links; the pages show otherwise. 99% of report and opinion steps already pointed at a document, but only at the **first** one the step prints. Many steps print several: 3,084 Senate and 1,037 Chamber opinion steps, and 1,225 Senate and 320 Chamber report steps (a .pdf with its .docx copy, or a report with its annex). The **registration step** carries what arrived with the bill: the Legislative Council's opinion (552 steps), the Government's decision (552) and the ordinance (389); the header lists the Economic and Social Council's opinion (550) and the Fiscal Council's (242), the Competition Council's and others, with no step of their own.
- **Stored:** `bill_step_documents (step_id, document_id, position)` (migration 0044), all documents of every step in the page's order, rewritten with the steps on each import (cascade). `bill_procedure_steps.document_id` stays as the first. On the dev copy: 40,725 links on 26,094 steps, at most 7 per step, none across bills. The updater's normal import writes them from now on.
- **What a document is** is read from the words of its label, then the file name, never from its content (`classifyBillDocument`, packages/parliament-model): committee report or opinion (the step says which, because the label is only the committee's name), Legislative Council, Economic and Social Council, Fiscal Council, Superior Council of Magistracy, Competition Council, any other "aviz" (the issuer is the words after it), the Government's view; the Government's decision, an ordinance, the initiator's form, the explanatory memorandum and the cover letters are recognised and kept out of the panel (the ordinance is Sprint 12b's).
- **The panel** (bill page, above the procedure): committee reports (verdict, registration number, amendments admitted and rejected, date, files), the Government's view, opinions of other bodies (in the document's own words, so no name is put in a case it was not printed in), committee opinions, and a closed list "requested, no answer on the official page" (a request with no received step for that committee or body; worded as what the page shows, not as a failure). A .pdf and its .doc/.docx copy are one line; the same opinion printed by both chambers' pages is one line. A bill whose dossier names none shows no panel. On the dev copy: 5,362 reports, 8,394 committee opinions, 5,109 opinions of other bodies (Legislative Council 2,192, Economic and Social Council 1,606, Fiscal Council 603, ...) and 935 Government views, on 2,200 bills.
- **The backfill is a narrow command**, `bills:dossiers:link-documents` (dry run, then `--persist`): it writes only the new table, matching stored steps by id, type and date; steps whose page changed since are left to the next import. It is not the full dossier import because that rewrites every bill (on the dev copy it would also correct 1,238 stored chamber-of-origin values, since dev is older than production).
- **Not done:** reading the files (amendments, 12d), the ordinance behind approval bills (12b), the priority list (12c).

### D-027 — How the updater works (2026-10-06, Sprint 9)
- **Where and when:** on the owner's Mac first, by hand (`ingest:updater:catch-up`), then once a day from a launchd job after a week of manual runs; the same code moves to the BC250 in Sprint 10. Each run catches up from the last successful one (D-007), so a missed day costs nothing.
- **One catch-up is an ordered list of steps**, each safe to repeat: (1) official vote lists for the days since the last run, (2) votes we lack, imported through the existing gates, (3) new bills and the dossiers that may have changed, (4) dossier import for only those pages, duplicate merge, gazette numbers, (5) read models, integrity, coverage numbers, cache purge, (6) a look at the presidential decrees for cabinet changes (report only).
- **Hold and publish (D-008) without a staging copy:** every item passes its own gate before it is written (a vote must agree with the official list, a dossier page must be a real page); a failed item is held, listed and not written. After the writes the integrity checks must not be worse than before the run; if they are, the run is held and the read models and caches are not refreshed. A held run opens a GitHub issue (or comments on the open one), never silently.
- **What is recorded** (migration 0041): `updater_runs` (steps, counts, held items, status), `worker_heartbeats` (is a worker alive), `updater_jobs` (a request waits until a worker takes it; the admin of Sprint 10 will write them), `data_revisions` (field, old value, new value, source for bill fates, stages, law and gazette numbers, and new votes and bills: the base of the later history feature).
- **Limits:** at most 150 requests per source per run at 3 s apart (the Chamber's bot protection answers a captcha after about 230); a run that finds the previous one still "running" for under 3 hours stops; over 3 hours it marks it failed and goes on.
- **Roster changes (added 6 Oct):** each run compares the official roster pages of the legislature (2 requests) with the profiles we hold; a member we hold no profile for is reported as a held item with the Sprint 6 steps to add them (crawl the profile, import, identity resolution) and is never imported automatically (a person is a sensitive record, D-014). Votes held for an unknown name are asked for again on every run until they are in, however long that takes (up to the 60-day cap).
- **Not in v1:** OCR, the admin screen (Sprint 10).

### D-023 — Individual votes use compact integer keys (2026-10-04, Sprint 3)

`individual_votes` was 175 MB for 253,130 rows (about 700 bytes a row: a 90-character text id, a 67-character vote id and three more text keys per row, five indexes, two of them never used). ### D-025 — How a bill's dossier is read and stored (2026-10-05, Sprint 7)

**Sources.** Each bill has up to two official pages: the Chamber's (`upl_pck2015.proiect?idp=`, found through the yearly list) and the Senate's (`Lista.aspx?an_cls=&nr_cls=`). Both are fetched once, saved raw under `data/coverage/raw/cdep-bill` and `senate-bill`, and read offline; a page that cannot be read is never saved (`bills:dossiers:fetch`, `bills:dossiers:import`). The pages of the bills we held before Sprint 7 had never been saved, so the whole legislature (about 4,000 pages) is fetched, not only the missing bills.

**One dossier from two pages.** Pages that share any identifier (PL-x, L, B number) are one bill. Each chamber's own page is authoritative for its own steps (the other page prints them in short), the Presidency's steps (promulgation, publication) come from both once, and the stage line is the one of the page that has seen the latest step. A bill with one page shows what that page says.

**What is stored, all as published.** `bill_dossiers` (one row per bill): the pages read and when, registration numbers (B.P.I., Chamber, Senate, the Government's letter), initiative type, urgency, the stage line, the Chamber's own summary of the object of regulation, the tacit-adoption deadline, and the fate. `bill_procedure_steps`: one row per step **and per committee** (a Chamber row that sends a bill to four committees becomes four steps), typed with the dossiers' own vocabulary (16 new values, old ones kept), with the committee or outside body, what a report, opinion or view concluded (`verdict`, its registration number, amendments admitted or rejected), the report and amendment deadlines, the vote counts printed beside an adoption, the vote page (linked to our vote when we hold it) and the stenogram. `bill_sponsors` gain the printed group and chamber. `bill_events` are rewritten from the steps because lists, member activity and the read models date a bill from them. Documents we already hold are never replaced (their extracted text hangs on them); new ones are added.

**Initiators are linked to members only when it is certain.** The Chamber page links each initiator's profile (legislature, chamber, number), which resolves to exactly one member (`members.source_ids.cdepProfileKey`). The Senate page prints names: a name resolves only when exactly one member of that chamber who sat on the bill's date has the same words in any order (a maiden-name suffix is tolerated); two members with the same words (Stoica Alin-Bogdan and Stoica Bogdan-Alin) stay unlinked, shown by name.

**Fate is read, not guessed.** `promulgated` needs a promulgation step or a law number on the page (decree number and date, law number, Official Gazette number and date when printed; the gazette number is on the Senate page). `rejected` only when the chamber that decides rejects (a first-chamber rejection passes the bill on). `withdrawn` when the initiator withdraws. `ended` only when the procedure ends without a hand-over to the other chamber. Anything else is `in_progress`, shown with the stage line as printed. Steps whose wording no rule recognises are typed `other`, kept in full, and listed by the import report so the rules can be extended; nothing is dropped or guessed.

**Not in this sprint:** amendments as texts, the content of opinions, Constitutional Court decisions behind a referral, and law diffs (F-feature work). `bills.status` now holds the stage line as printed.

### D-024 — How leadership roles are stored (2026-10-05, Sprint 6)

`member_roles` holds three kinds: `group` (leader, deputy leader, secretary of a parliamentary group), `bureau` (a chamber's Permanent Bureau, including its president) and `other` (retired: the undated "leader since the legislature began" rows of the old roster pipeline, deleted when a member is re-imported). Dates are the source's own and keep month precision. A role with no end in a **finished** legislature is closed at that legislature's end; in the current one it stays open. Official activity counts a chamber publishes about a member (initiatives, speeches, questions, e-vote attendance) go to `member_official_activity` **as published**, with the date they were read and the source page; we show them beside our own counts and never recompute or blend them. Deputies' counts need several requests per deputy and wait for the MP-enrichment feature work (F-3).
